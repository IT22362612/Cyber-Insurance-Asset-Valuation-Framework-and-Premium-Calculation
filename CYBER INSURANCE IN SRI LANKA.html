About the Research

Bridging the Gap in
Cyber Risk Pricing

The rapid digital transformation of the banking sector has significantly increased reliance on digital assets, making them a critical component of organizational value. However, the growing frequency and sophistication of cyber threats have exposed financial institutions to substantial risks, particularly concerning data breaches, system downtime, and financial losses.

This research focuses on developing a framework for Asset Valuation and Premium Calculation along with Risk Exposure modelling. The study specifically targets the banking sector in Sri Lanka, addressing the lack of standardized methodologies for quantifying the value of assets in cyber risk contexts.

The proposed solution integrates financial, operational, and risk-based factors to estimate the value and map it to insurance models. Ultimately, this research aims to enhance decision-making in cyber risk management and contribute to the development of a sustainable cyber insurance ecosystem.

Research Domain

Academic Foundation

The rapid digitization of organizational operations, particularly in the banking sector, has significantly increased exposure to cyber threats. Modern banking systems rely heavily on interconnected platforms and large volumes of sensitive data, making cyber incidents more frequent and impactful. Threats such as ransomware, data breaches, phishing, and system compromises can disrupt business continuity, damage customer trust, and result in substantial financial and regulatory consequences.

This trend is also evident in Sri Lanka, where banks and other institutions have experienced notable cyber incidents involving large-scale data exposure. Such events highlight the growing need for robust risk management frameworks to minimize potential losses and organizational damage.

While banks implement various security controls to protect their assets, completely preventing cyberattacks remains challenging. As a result, risk management has become essential, offering structured strategies such as risk avoidance, reduction, acceptance, and transfer. Among these, cyber insurance has emerged as an effective mechanism for transferring financial risk to insurers.

Given the multidimensional nature of cyber risks, effective premium calculation requires a structured evaluation of all asset categories including hardware, software, and data along with overall risk exposure. This study focuses on examining valuation methodologies for these asset categories to support more accurate and reliable cyber insurance premium calculations.

Limited Regional Research

Cyber insurance research and practical implementations remain limited, especially in developing countries like Sri Lanka. Most existing studies are based on developed regions with advanced cybersecurity infrastructures, which do not fully reflect the regulatory, technological, and operational realities of the Sri Lankan banking sector.

Limitations in Current Approaches

Current premium calculation approaches rely on high-level, generic security parameters and often overlook the importance of asset valuation. These models typically use simplified or static assumptions, despite the constantly evolving nature of cyber threats.

Lack of Structured Mathematical Models

The limited integration of structured mathematical models reduces the accuracy and transparency of premium calculations. There is a clear need for a comprehensive, parameter-driven framework that combines asset valuation with dynamic cyber risk assessment.

Inaccurate cyber insurance premium calculations can lead to significant financial risks and unfair outcomes. Excessively high premiums may discourage banks from obtaining coverage, leaving them fully exposed to cyber threats. Underestimated premiums can result in insufficient risk transfer, increasing the likelihood of financial loss.

The problem is structured into four key components: hardware valuation, software valuation, data valuation, and risk exposure modelling. Several challenges currently limit the effectiveness of cyber insurance models:

  • The absence of a standardized and widely accepted model for premium calculation
  • Inadequate asset valuation approaches, particularly for data one of the most valuable yet least understood assets
  • Lack of proper data classification prior to valuation, leading to inaccurate or uniform valuation of diverse data types
  • Insufficient incorporation of security-related parameters, resulting in limited assessment of risk factors
  • Minimal consideration of local context most existing models are designed for global markets
  • Reliance on static models that fail to adapt to the dynamic nature of cyber threats
  • Limited use of structured mathematical models for both asset valuation and premium calculation

Main Objective

To develop a fair and accurate cyber insurance premium calculation model integrated with asset valuation for the banking sector. The study aims to incorporate security-related parameters, quantify the value of organizational assets, and establish a mathematical model or set of equations for calculating premium values in a structured and consistent manner.

Specific Objectives

  • Identify existing models used for data valuation and cyber insurance premium calculation, along with their limitations
  • Understand and analyze the business processes of the banking sector in Sri Lanka
  • Identify the types of assets owned by banks in Sri Lanka and classify them in a structured manner
  • Develop a classification scheme for data assets based on sensitivity and importance
  • Define appropriate criteria for valuing each category of data assets
  • Assess and quantify potential risks associated with each data category
  • Develop mathematical equations to determine asset values and calculate insurance premiums
  • Validate the proposed model through expert evaluation to ensure accuracy, practicality, and alignment with industry requirements

The overall system framework consists of four key components: hardware valuation, software valuation, data valuation, and risk exposure identification. This study follows a structured and research-driven approach to develop an integrated framework.

An extensive review of academic literature, industry reports, and relevant standards was conducted to identify gaps and limitations in existing valuation and premium calculation models.

Asset Classification

To address the lack of standardized asset classification, relevant asset types within the banking sector were identified through analysis of publicly available banking information. This was validated using a questionnaire distributed among professionals in the Sri Lankan banking sector.

Quantitative Model Development

Based on the classification, quantitative models were developed to determine the value of each asset category. These calculated values are then used as inputs for the cyber insurance premium calculation model, incorporating security-related parameters to adjust premiums according to the organization's security posture.

Regulatory Integration

Key parameters and regulatory considerations were derived from the Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of Sri Lanka and Business Continuity Management System guidelines. The proposed parameters were validated through consultations with IT auditors.

Model-Driven Design Approach

This research adopts a model-driven design approach rather than relying on specific software tools or technologies. The focus is on the conceptual development of a cyber insurance premium calculation framework through extensive research and analysis.

Research Methodology

Parameters were identified and derived through comprehensive literature reviews, expert consultations with IT auditors in the Sri Lankan banking sector, questionnaire-based data collection, and analysis of regulatory frameworks including the Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of Sri Lanka.
